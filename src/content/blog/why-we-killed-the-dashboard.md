Most analytics dashboards are anxiety machines. You open them on Monday, see fourteen charts in twelve shades of blue, and leave knowing less than when you arrived.

The problem is not that data is bad. The problem is that **dashboards optimise for looking busy**, not for deciding.

## Three numbers beat fourteen charts

When we killed our internal dashboard, we kept three numbers on a single page:

1. **Activation** — did the user reach the “aha” moment in the first session?
2. **Retention week-1** — did they come back in the next seven days?
3. **Revenue per active** — are the people who stay actually paying?

Everything else was noise. Funnels still existed. Cohorts still existed. They lived one click deeper, for the moments when the three numbers moved and we needed a reason.

## Anxiety is a product decision

A 14-tab dashboard trains the team to check, not to act. Every morning becomes a ritual of scrolling past the same charts hoping something “interesting” appears. That is not insight. That is procrastination with better fonts.

We replaced the dashboard with a daily Slack message:

> Activation +2.1% · W1 retention −0.4% · RPA flat

If nothing moved more than a threshold, the message said “quiet day.” People stopped opening the tool out of habit. They opened it when a number broke.

## How to start

You do not need a new product. You need a ruthless list.

- Pick **three** metrics that, if they moved, would change what you ship this week.
- Put them on one screen. No filters, no date-range theatre for day one.
- Delete or hide everything else for thirty days.
- Reintroduce a chart only when someone can name the decision it unlocked.
